NEW R2 IMPLEMENTATIONS (2026-11-11)

These methods were implemented in this run after the source supplied by the
authors and the public main branch were found not to contain the R1 additions.
They are new, fully specified experiments, not recovery of historical code or
verification of the rounded R1 AP=0.969 values. They replace that incomplete
comparison with rerunnable evidence.

Common inputs
Only the original 14 observed features and training S are passed to the PU
learners. StandardScaler is fitted to the training rows. Logistic class models
use all 14 standardized features plus an intercept. No test Y, latent typicality,
known propensity, college, or frugal membership is used for fitting. Original
HGB results remain separate. Fully labeled HGB and logistic learners are named
true-label REFERENCES; neither defines a universal ceiling.

SAR_EM
Source: Bekker, Robberechts and Davis, Beyond the Selected Completely at Random
Assumption for Learning from Positive and Unlabeled Data. ECML PKDD 2019
proceedings, published 2020, pp. 71-85. doi:10.1007/978-3-030-46147-8_5.
Author code: https://github.com/ML-KULeuven/SAR-PU
Inspected commit: 6e4fc3d8c84ac3512669a4e36ffcb5086f5b42a7
Relevant function: sarpu/sarpu/pu_learning.py, pu_learn_sar_em.
MIT license supplied in SAR_PU_LICENSE.txt.

The reimplementation follows weighted balanced initialization, alternating
logistic M-steps and r=S+(1-S)f(1-e)/(1-fe). The propensity features, in order, are
monthly_total_spend, avg_meal_price, meals_per_day, breakfast_rate,
weekend_on_campus, canteen_ratio, snack_drink_ratio, low_price_window_ratio,
supermarket_spend. This 9-feature restriction is a new explicit R2 choice; the
missing R1 subset is not inferred from the old reported results.

M-steps minimize summed weighted logistic loss plus ||coefficient||^2/2, C=1;
the intercept is not penalized. They use L-BFGS-B with maxiter=200, ftol=1e-12,
gtol=1e-7 and maxls=40. Fractional responses are equivalent to duplication into
positive and negative cases with their corresponding weights; this equivalence
is numerically checked against sklearn. Iterations stop after at least 12
updates when mean absolute propensity slope in the last 10 updates <1e-4 and
the authors' expected-complete-log-likelihood window criterion <1e-4, or at 500.

The final inverse-propensity classifier refit is disabled, corresponding to the
authors' supported refit_classifier=False option. This avoids undocumented
handling of negative risk weights in an old liblinear wrapper. Other explicit
differences from that historical wrapper are standardized inputs, an unpenalized
intercept, and a current high-accuracy optimizer. This is a specified adaptation,
not a claim of bitwise reproduction of the authors' old software.

R1_IndexEM
This is the SAME EM estimator as above, but its propensity model receives only
H=( -z_spend -z_price +z_meals +z_breakfast +z_weekend +z_canteen
    -z_snack +z_low_price -z_supermarket )/9.
z denotes training-standardized observed features. Its logistic intercept and
slope are freely estimated; there is no sign constraint. Equal signs/weights
are fixed from the directions stipulated in the original feature generator.
They are not learned from test performance or individual hidden labels.
This matches the STYLE described for the restricted-index method in R1 but
does not recover its unknown exact signs, weights, subset, or initialization.
It is called Index-EM, not LBE, to avoid misattributing this restriction.

LBE_LF
Primary paper: Gong et al., Instance-Dependent Positive and Unlabeled Learning
with Labeling Bias Estimation, IEEE TPAMI, doi:10.1109/TPAMI.2021.3061456.
Author manuscript: https://gcatnjust.github.io/ChenGong/paper/gong_tpami21.pdf
Equations 8-14 and Algorithm 1 define logistic class and propensity models and
EM/Adam learning. Both models here receive ALL 14 features plus an intercept.
Initialize the classifier using naive logistic regression on S (C=1) and the
propensity coefficients at zero. Each full-batch iteration computes r, then
updates both coefficient vectors by Adam on negative expected log likelihood.
There is no L2 penalty after initialization. Learning rate=.001, beta1=.9,
beta2=.999, epsilon=1e-8. The maximum is 20,000 updates. Starting after 1,000,
check every 100 updates: absolute observed-log-likelihood change <1e-7 and
infinity norm of normalized gradient <1e-5. Gradients are checked against
finite differences of the observed-data likelihood. Adam is not guaranteed to
increase likelihood on every update; decreases and convergence are recorded.
This is our explicit linear implementation, not the authors' original code.
No correctness-of-specification or identifiability claim follows from high AP.

Outputs and fair comparison
Each method's primary score is f(X), evaluated on full test students and on U
separately. An additional score f(1-e)/(1-fe) is saved as *_UPosterior, clearly
separate from the main comparison. The logistic references use C=1 whereas
LBE's fitted likelihood is unpenalized, so these comparisons match a model
family, not every regularization/optimization choice. The known population
posterior is simulator-assisted and is not counted as a deployable PU method.

All 1,700 paired main/beta/mechanism/stress/alternative-generator cases run
the three estimators plus five logistic versions of the original strategies.
Convergence flags are included for every fit, with no deletion of poor-performing
cohorts or selection of a method using test Y.

Numerical detail for supplementary *_UPosterior scores: the denominator 1-f*e
is floored at 1e-15. These are model-based scores, not known population
conditionals. The primary benchmark uses f(X) directly.
